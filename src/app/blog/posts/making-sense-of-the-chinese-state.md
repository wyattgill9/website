# Making Sense of the Chinese State (November 2026)

_One diagram of who sits above whom in China: the Party centre, the state organs the National People's Congress elects, the advisory CPPCC, and the society the Party leads. Click any box to see its links._

---

China's state constitution describes a legislature, a government, courts and a head of state. The Party's constitution describes a second hierarchy that leads all of them. The diagram below draws both, top to bottom, and shows where they meet.

Each coloured block is one system: Party Central, the National People's Congress, the Presidency, the State Council, the military, supervision, the courts, the procuratorates, the CPPCC and the social sphere. Grey lines show the hierarchy: who elects, appoints or contains whom. Click a box and its other relationships appear as coloured arrows: Party leadership, personnel, elections, oversight, representation, funding and shared personnel. ## The Party at the top

The chart starts with the **National Congress of the CCP**, which meets every five years and elects the Central Committee and the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection. The Central Committee elects the Politburo, its Standing Committee and the General Secretary. The General Secretary must be a Standing Committee member and presides over the Central Secretariat, the working body of the Politburo and its Standing Committee.

Below the Central Committee, the chart follows the Party's own categories, set out in the Regulations on CCP Working Bodies (revised November 2025). **Decision-making and coordinating bodies** are the commissions for deepening reform, national security, cyberspace, finance and foreign affairs, among others, plus leading groups for education, rural work, Taiwan, and Hong Kong and Macao. **Functional departments** are Organization, Publicity, United Front Work, International, Political and Legal Affairs, and Social Work. **Administrative offices** are the standing offices of the commissions. Then come two **dispatched bodies**, the **public institutions** the Party runs directly, such as the Central Party School and People's Daily, and three **subordinate bodies**: the archives, secrecy and cryptography offices.

## One institution, two names

Many boxes carry a second name. The Office of the Central Cyberspace Affairs Commission is also the Cyberspace Administration of China. The Taiwan Work Office is also the State Council's Taiwan Affairs Office. The Central Party School is the National Academy of Governance. The Publicity Department carries the nameplates of the State Council Information Office, the National Press and Publication Administration and the China Film Administration. The United Front Work Department carries those of the National Religious Affairs Administration and the Overseas Chinese Affairs Office, and leads the National Ethnic Affairs Commission.

The State Council's own administrative offices have shrunk to one, its Research Office. The others are nameplates of Party organs. This is the plainest form of Party leadership over the state: one office answers to a Party commission and presents a government face. The diagram draws each such body once, in the Party block, and gives its second name in the panel.

## The state the NPC elects

On paper, the National People's Congress is the highest organ of state power. It elects the President, the chairman of the Central Military Commission, the director of the National Supervisory Commission, the president of the Supreme People's Court and the Procurator-General, and it decides on the Premier. That is why the state blocks hang from it. The NPC Standing Committee, the State Council, the CPPCC, the Supreme Court and the Supreme Procuratorate each have a Party group, and the Politburo Standing Committee hears their work reports.

The State Council works under a premier-responsibility system. Below the Premier sit the vice premiers and state councilors; a secretary-general and the General Office; 26 constituent departments (21 ministries, three commissions, the People's Bank of China and the National Audit Office); 17 state bureaus, each run by a ministry or commission; SASAC, the one "special institution", which exercises ownership rights over the central state firms; 14 institutions directly under the State Council, from Customs to the financial regulator; and 7 public institutions such as Xinhua and the Chinese Academy of Sciences. Hong Kong and Macao hang below it. Each has a Chief Executive, a legislature, a judiciary and a box of central government offices, including the Liaison Office and the PLA garrison.

The Central Military Commission appears once. The Party CMC and the state CMC have the same members: one body with two names. Since April 2024 the PLA has had four services (Army, Navy, Air Force and Rocket Force) and four arms (Aerospace, Cyberspace, Information Support and Joint Logistic Support). Since 2018 the People's Armed Police, which now includes the China Coast Guard, has answered to the Central Committee and the CMC rather than the State Council.

The National Supervisory Commission sits under the NPC, but it shares one set of offices with the Party's discipline commission. Its reach covers all public officials exercising public power, not only Party members. The two are drawn in different blocks because one is elected by the Party Congress and the other by the NPC. Click either to see the link between them.

## The CPPCC: a channel, not a legislature

The Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference is an advisory, united-front body, and its 34 sectors are the clearest map of whom the Party consults. When the 14th National Committee was formed in January 2023 it had 2,172 members, and 2,125 by the March 2026 session. The sectors break down as follows: 544 seats for the CCP, the eight legal non-communist parties and non-party figures; 314 for seven people's organizations such as the trade unions and the Women's Federation; 1,009 for thirteen occupational and social "circles"; 69 for religious circles; and 236 for specially invited people, 124 of them from Hong Kong.

Many of these bodies reach the state only through their CPPCC seats. The eight democratic parties, the national religious organizations and the occupational circles have no other place in the formal state, so the diagram draws them inside the CPPCC block. A Party Leadership Group sits at the top of that block, reporting to the Politburo Standing Committee.

## The social sphere

Business associations, social organizations and private firms are not CPPCC units. They meet the Party-state through registration with civil affairs departments, through sponsoring units under the "dual management" system, and since 2023 through Party-building led by the Central Social Work Department. Since a March 2026 amendment to the social-groups regulation, industry management departments also oversee industry associations and chambers of commerce. Inside non-public firms the Party works through its own organizations, and the 2026 Opinions on social work single out internet platform companies.

## How it was checked

The structure was audited on 19 November 2026 against official texts: the [2023 institutional reform plan](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/2023-03/16/content_5747072.htm), the [State Council's 2023 notice on its institutions](https://www.mee.gov.cn/zcwj/gwywj/202303/t20230320_1021204.shtml), the [Regulations on CCP Working Bodies](https://www.news.cn/politics/20251207/419e6cd67fb04e37a7815f984d308e62/c.html), the Party and CPPCC constitutions, the Organic Laws and the [14th CPPCC roster](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2023/01-18/9937768.shtml). The audit moved several things. The discipline commission had been drawn under the NPC, but it is elected by the Party Congress. The Cyberspace Administration had been drawn as a commission of its own. The 17 state bureaus had been nested under the ministries, although seven of them are run by commissions, the central bank or the market regulator.

Some limits remain. The Party does not publish a complete list of its leading groups, so the chart shows only those confirmed in reform plans or appointments. The per-sector CPPCC seats come from the January 2023 roster, the last published breakdown. The report that each Liaison Office doubles as the Party's Hong Kong or Macao work committee rests on secondary sources. And the chart shows institutions, not the people who run them.
